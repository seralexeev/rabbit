# 2026-10-03 Body PCB rev B

Offline (the robot was off). Revision B of the deck-2 board, generated by `pcb/rabbit-body/gen/`. Russian report with the tables: `reports/2026-10-03-body-pcb-rev-b.md`. Rev A (RoboClaw, Pololu modules, ATO fuses, INA4235) stays in git at `f36dd87`. No robot code, Forge or `cad/brackets` changed.

## Result

- Same outline, holes, 45 + 45 mm stack and 11.1 × 18 mm ZED cut-out; everything on the top side; 4 layers 1 oz / 0.5 oz.
- The motor drive is on the board: STM32G474RET6 (LQFP-64; STM32G431RBT6 fits the same footprint) + 2 × DRV8316CR in 3x PWM mode. BLDC-first after the owner's decision (Q39, maxon ECX / Faulhaber BX4–BXT class with Halls and an integrated encoder): three phases, three Hall inputs and an A/B/I encoder per motor; a brushed motor still fits on OUTA–OUTB. Encoder connectors are JST GH 7-pin (5 V, 3.3 V, GND, A, B, I, GND): A/B on timer encoder inputs on 5 V-tolerant pins (left TIM4 PB6/PB7, right TIM3 PB4/PB5; LQFP-64 has TIM5 only on pins taken by the ADC and USART), I on PB3 / PC9; the same lines can read an SSI encoder by GPIO. Hall inputs on separate PH connectors, a hardware brake chopper, an SWD header, USART2 to the Pi's UART0 (RoboClaw packet-serial subset), BOOT0/NRST on Pi GPIO0/1. Solder jumpers send UART0 to a RoboClaw header instead.
- E-stop in hardware: GPIO16 → loop jumper J5 → `ESTOP` with 10 kΩ to ground on the driver side → TIM1/TIM8 break inputs (low-side brake at once, no code) and, through 100k/2.2 µF (0.23–0.30 s) and a charge-pump "MCU alive" detector, a Schmitt NAND → DRVOFF of both drivers (all six FETs off; ~10 ms if the MCU hangs).
- Power: LM74800 ideal diode + switch (2 × NCEP4090GU, 40 V 2.2 mΩ) with a soft latch (button → PNP → EN; VBUS holds EN through an 8.2 V zener down to 9.6 V; GPIO17 `gpio-poweroff` pulls EN low and an RC keeps it low 1.1 s after the Pi dies); SMD NANO2 fuses (15/10/5/5 A); 2 × LM61495 (5.17 V body, 6 V servo); the Jetson straight from the bus through a TPS16630 eFuse (4.6 A, 9.4 V UVLO, 19.2 V OVP) — its barrel takes 9–20 V; 4 × INA226 with ALERT wired to GPIO24 (P5 closed).
- Grounds: In1 is solid GND except a GND_MOT island under the drivers, bulk caps and brake (also filled on F.Cu and B.Cu; the autorouter sees only 6.4 × 8.4 mm of it under each DRV8316 on B.Cu and the rest is filled after routing, so signal vias can pass under the MOT_BUS bar); it meets GND only at NT1 by the XT60 minus.
- LM61495 placement follows SNVSBZ4A figure 11-2: 470 nF caps right at the VIN/PGND pairs, 10 µF outside them, an 0402 CBOOT beside RBOOT/CBOOT with SW brought out between VIN2 and RBOOT, CBIAS at BIAS. The first layout had the boot cap 5 mm away under the control row and the router could not connect it; `rabbit-body.kicad_dru` allows the courtyard overlap inside these groups only.
- Every part has an LCSC number and JLC stock for 3 × two boards; JLC places all SMT and THT parts. Nothing is hand-soldered on the board.

## Decisions and why

- **Quality parts by rule (owner, 03.10): money is not a constraint.** Infineon BSC010N04LS / BSC026N04LS instead of NCE MOSFETs, Littelfuse TVS, Panasonic EEH-ZK hybrid-polymer (AEC-Q200) bulk caps, Coilcraft XAL1010 inductors, Vishay WSL shunts, Molex KK 396 phase headers (genuine JST VH is not at JLC), Würth headers, Phoenix terminal, Nexperia PNP; all JLC-stocked. List in the report §5.1.
- **F2 8 A** (BLDC report §8.5 asks 7.5 A; NANO2 has 7 and 8 A): two Pololu motors at the 4 A firmware peak stay within it; stalls are the job of the firmware current limit and the DRV8316 OCP, the fuse is for shorts.

- **Stock: ask JLC, not the LCSC shop page.** Rev A's blocker "PCA9685 0 pcs" was the LCSC shop; JLC's assembly stock had 393 (and 1 205 of the Q900 grade). `gen/stock.py` queries `selectSmtComponentList` for every LCSC number and also caught six wrong codes in my own tables (E96 resistors and a zener whose numbers pointed at other values) — check the returned model string, not just the stock.
- **TVS clamp, not battery voltage, sets the part ratings.** SMBJ20A/CA clamp at 32.4 V. That rules out the 28 V TPS56637, the 23 V TPS259474 and the 30 V CSD17573Q5B suggested earlier; LM61495 (36/42 V), TPS16630 (67 V), LM74800 (65 V), DRV8316 (40 V), INA226 (40 V) and 40 V FETs stay above it.
- **No 12 V converter for the Jetson.** Its carrier takes 9–20 V; a buck from a 12.4–16.8 V battery runs in dropout anyway. An eFuse gives the EN from the Pi, a current limit and an overvoltage cut.
- **Ideal diode at the input.** It blocks reverse current, so regen never reaches the V-mount BMS; the electronics and the 17.67 V brake chopper absorb it.
- **Brake chopper powered from the motor bus** (LM393 + TL431), so it works with the MCU hung and the 5 V buck down (e.g. the robot pushed while off).
- **PCA9685 kept** (in stock at JLC) so steering works before the motor firmware exists; S0 can be moved to the MCU's TIM17 by a solder jumper.
- **No USB hub.** With the RoboClaw gone the Pi needs two ports (flash drive, Wi-Fi), ~1 A of the ~1.2 A the Pi's ports give. P14 closed.
- **INA226 VBUS tied to the adjacent IN−** (the load side of each shunt behind its 10 Ω filter: ~20 µA, 0.2 mV error) instead of a separate track to the bus: on all four the VBUS pin sat on the far side of the package from its bus.
- **LM61495 RT 39 kΩ to ground (414 kHz)** instead of RT to VCC (400 kHz): the VCC connection would have crossed FB and AGND under the package.
- **Spare battery output dropped.** No room next to VBUS without crossing the motor feed; the motor-bus terminal J49 (RoboClaw fallback) doubles as one.

## Toolchain lessons

- **TI land-pattern dimensions in parentheses are centre-to-centre.** I read "(4.8)" and "(2.78)" as outer extents, so the DRV8316 and LM74800 pads overlapped their exposed pads; DRC caught it as shorts to the EP. The LM61495 RPH pads (notched corners) were measured off the vector drawing instead.
- **KiCad rule areas forbid tracks of every net, including the hand-drawn ones of the pour's own net**, and the DSN export drops track-only rule areas anyway, so the pours have no keepouts: the planes themselves keep Freerouting out.
- **KiCad exports a zone to the DSN as a plane by its outline, not its fill.** Pads of other nets inside a pour outline (latch resistors in VBUS_RAW, the Jetson INA226 in 6V_RAW, decoupling grounds in VBUS) were unreachable: 385 violations and 85 unrouted after 12 passes. `autoroute.py` now replaces the planes with the filled copper. That exposed the second half: a plane on a signal layer is no obstacle to Freerouting, which then ran signal tracks through the MOT_BUS bar and the buck inputs and the refill split them; each pour now also gets a keepout 0.35 mm inside its copper. {{ROUTE_RESULT}}
- **After `board.Remove(zone)`, zone objects from the same session come back as bare `SwigPyObject`s.** Read everything needed from the zones (filled polygons) before removing the GND fills.
- **Freerouting rejects overlapping areas on a power layer.** The In1 GND zone now has the GND_MOT island cut out as a hole.
- **Ground fan-out must check pad rectangles, not circles**, or fine-pitch IC grounds never get a via.
- **Freerouting does not route pads to a plane on a signal layer.** Every connection from a pad to a pour (LED resistors, INA226 supply pins, the buck feedback/BIAS networks, the LDO and button 5 V, the J49 return, VREF to AVDD) is a hand track or via in `routing.py`; the router then routes around them.
- **Overlapping zones of one net with different priorities do not connect in KiCad 10:** the lower one is knocked out with the zone clearance, so the motor-bus corridor stopped 0.3 mm short of the bar and the front VBUS pieces floated. `routing.py` merges each net's polygons per layer into one zone.
- **Never edit `make.sh` while it runs** (`sh` reads the script as it goes).
- Freerouting on this board: ~3 min per pass (rev A: seconds).
