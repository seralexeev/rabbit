# nvblox mapping inside the camera process

Date: 2026-10-02, 06:38–11:22 UTC. Uncommitted at the time of writing. Earlier history: an nvblox_torch pipeline existed from 2025-08-16 to 2026-04 and was removed on 2026-10-01 in favour of ZED spatial mapping (`2026-10-01-zed-spatial-mapping.md`).

## Why

ZED spatial mapping held about 2 GB of RAM on an 8 GB board, never corrected the mesh after loop closures, never forgot and stopped integrating at its memory cap. The planner needed a 2D traversability grid that took 0.2–3 s to build on the CPU from the mesh.

## Benchmark on a recorded SVO

- `rabbit.zed.record {"name": ...}` records an SVO2 inside the running camera process (LOSSLESS, about 1 GB/min). `loop2.svo2`: two 1.2 m loops driven by a mission, 66.5 s.
- Replayed through the SDK in several configurations (15 fps replay; decoding the SVO itself costs much of the CPU):

  | Config | fps | process CPU | RSS |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | no depth, no tracking | 15.7 | 144 % | 165 MB |
  | NEURAL_LIGHT depth | 15.6 | 148 % | 393 MB |
  | + GEN_1 tracking | 15.5 | 193 % | — |
  | + GEN_3 tracking | 15.1 | 186 % | 980 MB |
  | + ZED mesh 5 cm | 14.7 | 191 % | 3057 MB |

- Exported as a 3DMatch dataset (1091 frames, 640×360) and fused with nvblox `fuse_3dmatch` (5 cm voxels, 4 m range, 2D ESDF slice 0.04–0.45 m): 21.0 s wall for 1091 frames, max RSS 184 MB, GPU 6 %; integrate 0.97 ms, ESDF 1.56 ms, mesh 9.7 ms per call.
- Map comparison through the same grid builder, one run so both share an origin: ZED 4988 occupied / 8085 free cells, nvblox 6869 / 9516. An earlier claim that the ZED mesh was offset 9 cm from the poses was wrong: it compared two runs with different origins.

## Implementation

- nvblox pinned to commit 23d35fc, built for CUDA 12.6 / sm_87 on the Jetson (about 16 minutes; 888 s inside the Docker build).
- `workspaces/rabbit/native/rabbit_nvblox/` (`module.cpp`, `depth.cu`, `CMakeLists.txt`): a nanobind extension. `Mapper.integrate(depth, fx, fy, cx, cy, world_from_camera, floor_y)`, `take_mesh_updates`, `save`/`load` (`room.nvblx`), `save_mesh`, `clearance_grid`, `clear_below`, `clear_outside`. It converts ZED frames (OpenGL camera, Y-up world) to nvblox (OpenCV camera, Z-up) and back. CMake needed imported targets for static glog/gflags and `find_package(CUDAToolkit)`.
- `docker/Dockerfile.zed` is multi-stage: the first stage builds nvblox and the extension into `/opt/rabbit_nvblox`. `.dockerignore` first excluded the native sources, so the first image build had nothing to compile.
- `zed.py`: ZED spatial mapping removed; a `_map_loop` worker thread integrates the latest depth+pose; mesh blocks go out in the existing chunk format only when they change, so the HUD, explore and Forge needed no change.
- **Clearance grid** `rabbit.map.grid` (09:53–10:20 UTC): ESDF slice between 0.04 and 0.45 m above the floor, int16 millimetres to the nearest obstacle (0 = obstacle, −1 = unknown), 5 cm cells, every ≤ 2 s while the map changes, plus a snapshot request. 1–5 ms on the GPU instead of 0.2–3 s on the CPU. Bugs on the way: a segfault from reading the device-memory slice on the host (fixed with a host copy); an empty grid after loading a map, because the incremental ESDF ignores loaded blocks (full-layer update after load, reset and floor changes: 237 ms once, then 1.1 ms incremental).
- **Map rebuilds after pose corrections**: frames are stored and the map is rebuilt when ZED moves keyframes by more than 5 cm or 2°. In testing it never triggered.
- **Reflections and noise** (09:05 UTC): glossy parquet and glass produced a mirrored room under the floor (vertices down to −1.2 m). The depth kernel clips rays at the floor plane and snaps points less than 3 cm above the floor onto it when that moves them less than 15 cm along the ray; after loading a map everything below the floor is cleared. Integration range 3.5 m, raised to 5.0 m on 2026-10-02 22:15 UTC so the kitchen island at 4.3 m enters the map.
- **Debris from pose jumps** (11:17 UTC): blocks up to 206 m and 1.57 km away inflated the grid until publishing failed. Fix: the grid is windowed ±25 m around the robot, blocks beyond 50 m are cleared, and integration is skipped when the implied speed exceeds 1.5 m/s.
- Static robot: integration was skipped while the camera was static, so a freshly reset map stayed empty ("no map from the camera"); now a frame is integrated at least every 2 s.
- A self-inflicted outage: enabling nvblox depth preprocessing (dilations) crashed the node on an internal size check; perception was down for about a minute.

## Result

`rabbit-zed` 121 % → about 90 % CPU driving and 3.38 → 1.36 GB RSS (07:58 UTC); used RAM on the Jetson 5283 → 3696 MB. nvblox integrates 640×360 depth in about 1 ms per frame. Decision on 08:53 UTC ("NV-blocks or the native SDK?"): keep the hybrid — ZED for depth, tracking and relocalization, nvblox for the map — because of memory (2 GB vs < 200 MB), a fuller map, the ability to rebuild after loop closure, and control over what goes on the wire. Downsides: a custom extension, a 15-minute cold build and a pinned commit.
