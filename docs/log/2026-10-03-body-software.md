# Rabbit 2.0 body software, written offline (2026-10-03)

The Rabbit 2.0 body (Raspberry Pi 4: safety loop, power supervisor, RPLIDAR C1, 4× VL53L8CX, bumpers, power button) was written and verified before any of its parts were bought, so that bring-up is wiring plus configuration. Design: [`reports/2026-10-03-architecture-2.0-body.md`](../reports/2026-10-03-architecture-2.0-body.md), pins in [`-wiring.md`](../reports/2026-10-03-architecture-2.0-wiring.md), sensor poses in `cad/brackets/README.md`. Nothing here ran on a Pi; the checks are unit tests, the full-stack simulator and a local Forge writer.

## What exists now

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `src/lib/rplidar.py` | Slamtec protocol: requests, response descriptors, the 5-byte legacy SCAN node, GET_INFO / GET_HEALTH / GET_SAMPLERATE payloads, a byte stream that resynchronises after bad bytes and back-dates every node by the bytes still behind it in the buffer, rotation assembly on the start flag |
| `src/node/lidar.py` (`rabbit-lidar`) | C1 on UART4 at 460800: STOP, GET_INFO, GET_SAMPLERATE, GET_HEALTH (RESET on `error`), SCAN; restarts the scan after 1 s without a rotation and reopens the port after three restarts; publishes `rabbit.lidar.scan` per rotation and `rabbit.health.lidar` at 1 Hz |
| `src/lib/lidar.py` | the scan wire format, mount (`LIDAR_MOUNT`), masked sectors (`LIDAR_MASK`), points in the base frame, per-point times and motion de-skew, per-sector minimums |
| `src/lib/tof.py`, `src/lib/tof_device.py`, `src/node/tof.py` (`rabbit-tof`) | VL53L8CX zone geometry (8×8, 45°, yaw/pitch/roll per mount), status filter, floor and overhead rejection, the `rabbit.tof` message; the device wrapper (real driver over smbus2, fake device); one thread per sensor, re-init after 0.5 s without a frame, power cycle of all four through `TOF_PWR_OFF` after three failures in a row (at most every 30 s) |
| `src/lib/safety_loop.py`, `src/node/safety.py` (`rabbit-safety`) | the independent safety loop: pure `SafetyLoop` (clock injected) and the node that wires NATS and GPIO |
| `src/lib/power_supervisor.py`, `src/node/power.py` (`rabbit-power`), `workspaces/body/rabbit-power.service` | the power state machine (pure) and the host service that drives the button, LEDs and Jetson EN |
| `src/node/jetson_power.py`, `workspaces/jetson/rabbit-jetson-power.service` | the agent on the Jetson host that powers it off or reboots it on `rabbit.power.jetson` |
| `src/lib/hardware.py` | GPIO pin map (wiring doc), `PiGpio` (libgpiod v2), `FakeGpio`, `FakeC1` (a C1 emulator that answers the protocol and streams nodes at 5 kHz), `RABBIT_HW=fake` |
| `workspaces/body/compose.yaml`, `body.env`, `nats-hub.conf`, `workspaces/jetson/nats-leaf.conf`, `docker/Dockerfile.body`, `scripts/body-setup.sh`, `scripts/deploy.sh --body` | the deploy shape for the Pi (not run) |

Changed: `roboclaw.py` and `steering.py` take `DRIVE_INPUT=safety` (listen only to `rabbit.safety.drive`); `ina.py` takes `I2C_BUS`; `CommandArbiter.decide()` takes the time so the safety loop reuses the exact joystick rule; `RabbitNode` got a `prepare()` hook that runs before NATS is reachable and a `connected` property (the power supervisor must work without NATS); the node start snapshot records `SAFETY_*`, `LIDAR_*`, `TOF_*`, `POWER_*`, `DRIVE_*`, `I2C_*`.

## Contracts

| Subject | Content |
|---|---|
| `rabbit.lidar.scan` | binary, one rotation: header `<qqIHHffff>` (40 B) `ts_start`, `ts_end` (ns, Pi clock), `seq`, `n`, flags (0), mount `x`, `y`, `z`, `yaw_deg`; then `n` × `<HH>` distance mm, angle·64 (device angle, clockwise seen from above). Only points with a return and outside the masked sectors; ~450–500 points, ~2 kB, 10 Hz |
| `rabbit.health.lidar` | 1 Hz: rate, points and measurements per rotation, rotation time, bad nodes, skipped bytes, short rotations, restarts, reconnects, device health, nearest return per 30° sector |
| `rabbit.tof` | JSON, **one message per sensor frame** (4 × 15 Hz): `sensor`, `seq`, `ts`, 64 `distance_mm`, 64 `status`, `points` (obstacles in the base frame, m), `nearest_m`, counts of valid, floor and overhead zones |
| `rabbit.health.tof` | 1 Hz: per sensor state, rate, frame age, zone counts, nearest, errors, resets, init time; power cycles |
| `rabbit.safety.drive` | 50 Hz `{ts, seq, speed, steer, source, cap_fwd, cap_rev, reason}`, the only command roboclaw and steering obey with `DRIVE_INPUT=safety` |
| `rabbit.safety.state` | 10 Hz: mode, binding reason, all reasons, requested and allowed command, caps and clearances per direction, E-stop line, self-test, bumpers, battery flags, power state, every input's age |
| `rabbit.safety.estop` / `.reset` | latched software E-stop `{source, reason}` and its release |
| `rabbit.power.state`, `rabbit.power.request`, `rabbit.power.jetson` | supervisor state at 1 Hz and on change; request-reply `{action: shutdown \| reboot_jetson \| power_cycle_jetson, source}`; the Pi asking the Jetson agent `{action: poweroff \| reboot}` |
| `rabbit.sim.gpio.in`, `rabbit.sim.gpio.out`, `rabbit.sim.setup` | simulator only: input levels for fake GPIO (bumpers), output levels from it (E-stop line), and a test hook to place the robot, add blocks with a height and switch the simulated lidar, ToF and bumpers |

Base frame for the body: rear axle centre on the floor, x forward, y left, z up (`lib/safety.free_distance` uses x forward, y right; `lidar.to_safety_frame` converts).

Two deviations from `-body.md`, both deliberate: `rabbit.tof` is JSON with one message per sensor instead of one binary message for all four (each sensor has its own frame time and freshness, a hung sensor does not delay the others, Forge and the HUD read JSON; ~1 kB per frame, ~60 kB/s), and it carries the filtered obstacle points next to the raw zones, so the safety loop and the simulator share one classification.

## Lidar

- **Protocol** from Slamtec's "RPLIDAR series protocol" LR001 v1.0 (request `A5 cmd [size payload xor]`, descriptor `A5 5A` + 30-bit length + 2-bit mode + type; SCAN `A5 5A 05 00 00 40 81`, GET_INFO `…14 00 00 00 04`, GET_HEALTH `…03 00 00 00 06`; node: byte 0 quality≪2 | S̄≪1 | S, byte 1 angle[6:0]≪1 | C, byte 2 angle[14:7], bytes 3–4 distance·4 little-endian; distance 0 = no return; ≥ 10 ms after STOP, ≥ 500 ms after RESET; RESET recovers the `error` health state) and checked against the SDK (`Slamtec/rplidar_sdk`, `sl_lidar_cmd.h`, `handler_normalnode.cpp`, `sl_lidar_driver.cpp`). The tests build packets from these bytes, including the EXPRESS_SCAN checksum example on p.18.
- **C1 specifics** (datasheet C1M1-R2 v1.0): UART 460800 8N1; the motor "can't start or stop alone, its working state depends on the laser scan command", so there is no motor command and no DTR; 5 kHz, 10 Hz, 0.05–12 m; Slamtec's ROS launch files use the "Standard" mode, which the SDK serves with the legacy SCAN (0x20). Whether SCAN really runs at 5 kHz on the C1 is checked at connect with GET_SAMPLERATE and logged in `lidar.connected`.
- **Angle zero.** Datasheet fig. 2-4: 0° is the direction the top-cap triangle points to, opposite the cable, and angles grow clockwise seen from above. The mast routes the cable out at the front, so the default mount yaw is **180°**, not the 0° written in `cad/brackets/README.md` (open-issues P16). Mount from the brackets: 159.7 mm ahead of the rear axle on the centreline, plane 230 mm.
- **Time.** The C1 sends no timestamps. Each read is stamped with `time.time_ns()` on return, and every node in it is back-dated by the bytes behind it (21.7 µs per byte) plus `LIDAR_LATENCY_MS` (0 until measured). `ts_start`/`ts_end` are the first and last measurement of the rotation; `lidar.point_times()` spreads the points by swept angle. `lidar.deskew(scan, poses_at)` takes a vectorised pose source and returns de-skewed points in its frame; nav uses it with its own pose track. At 0.5 m/s a rotation smears 5 cm; the test shows the de-skewed wall flat within 4 mm.

## ToF

- **Driver.** No maintained pip package for the VL53L8CX on Linux besides `circuitpython-vl53lxcx` 0.0.5 (sensebox, a Python port of ST's ULD that carries the 86 kB firmware). It has three problems on a Pi, found by reading its source: the firmware path is built by cutting `__file__` at the first dot, which breaks under `python3.x` (reproduced); 8-bit arrays (`target_status`, `nb_target_detected`) skip ST's 4-byte swap, so zone *i* is at *i* XOR 3; its poll loops retry 200 times without sleeping, where ST waits 10 ms per try. `lib/tof_device.py` subclasses the base class with smbus2 `i2c_rdwr` transfers on `/dev/i2c-N` (no Blinka), patches the firmware path, reorders the byte arrays and polls like ST. ST's own driver is C (STSW-IMG040); a C port is the fallback if this one misbehaves on the real sensor.
- **Geometry** (`cad/brackets/README.md`): front sensors 224.6 mm ahead, ±19 mm, yaw ±22°; rear 51.4 mm behind, yaw 180 ∓ 22°; 57.8 mm high, pitch +15°, and the boards lie landscape (roll 90°). Zone order per ST DS14161 §7.1: the lens flips the image, zone 0 sees the top right of the scene; columns are flipped by default (`TOF_FLIP_COLS=1`), rows and the roll sign are config. Distance is taken as perpendicular to the sensor (ULD "rad2perp"), `TOF_DISTANCE=radial` switches it. All of this needs the hand test on hardware (V8).
- **Filtering.** Valid: status 5 or 9 (ST: 5 is 100 %, 9 and 6 50 %; 6 occurs on the first frame), at least one target, 2 cm–4 m. A point lower than 3 cm is floor, higher than 0.26 m (robot top 0.24 m) is overhead. On a flat floor the bottom rows see the floor from ~0.44 m and nothing is reported; a wall 0.3 m ahead becomes points 0.3 m ahead at 3–26 cm height.

## Safety loop

50 Hz plus an immediate step on every bumper or INA ALERT edge. Inputs and freshness exactly as in `-body.md`: command 0.3 s, brain (`rabbit.nav.state`) 0.5 s, lidar 0.3 s, each ToF 0.25 s, RoboClaw 0.3 s, INA 1 s.

- **Arbitration:** hard stops (software E-stop, power shutting down, RoboClaw lost, overcurrent, self-test not passed) > bumpers > joystick (`CommandArbiter`: owns the motors 1 s after active input) > `rabbit.cmd.drive`. The joystick is limited like nav.
- **Clearance:** `free_distance` of the 2.0 footprint (front 0.2452, rear 0.072, half width 0.102, margin 0.02) along the commanded arc, forward and in reverse, against fresh lidar and ToF points (points inside the body are dropped). Allowed speed is the largest v with 0.05 + 0.25·v + v²/2 ≤ clearance, cut to 0 below 3 cm/s; caps are per direction, so moving away is always allowed.
- **Per fault** (the table in `-body.md`): lidar stale → 0.1 m/s both ways; a ToF stale → 0.08 m/s toward its end while the lidar is fresh, closed otherwise; bumper → zero at once, its end closed until released + 1 s; INA stale → 0.1 m/s; battery mean ≤ 13.2 V over 5 s → half speed; ≤ 12.8 V for 10 s → stop; battery ≥ 13 A or motors ≥ 8 A (polled, or the ALERT pin with `SAFETY_INA_ALERT=1`) → E-stop line low for 2 s after the last sample; RoboClaw silent 0.3 s → line low until 1 s of fresh telemetry with zero PWM; `rabbit.safety.estop` latches until `rabbit.safety.reset`; power `stopping` and later → line low.
- **E-stop line** (GPIO16 high = run). Low from start; the self-test waits until every enabled input is fresh, checks that the RoboClaw reports E-stop (status bit 0x1) while the line is low, raises it and checks the flag clears (3 s each); otherwise `estop_line_fault` and no motion until restart.
- **Choices where `-body.md` is silent** (all pinned by tests, open-issues Q28): "crawl off the bumper ≤ 0.1 m/s, 0.5 m" means the other end is capped at 0.1 m/s while the bump is active and closes after 0.5 m of crawling with the bumper still pressed (a broken bumper wire must not let the robot wander off blind); "50 % speed" halves the command; a lost brain rejects only commands with `source` `nav`; shadow mode (`SAFETY_MODE=shadow`) computes, publishes and records everything but never drives the line; sensors enter the loop one by one through `SAFETY_SENSORS` (empty until each one ran in shadow, stage 8).
- **Stall** (stage 6, encoders) is not implemented: the encoders don't count yet.

## The GPIO line does not drop when the process dies

`-body.md` and `-wiring.md` assume "the kernel releases the line, the 1 kΩ pull-down stops the RoboClaw". On current Raspberry Pi kernels that is false: `pinctrl-bcm2835.c` has `persist_gpio_outputs`, **true by default** in rpi-6.6 and rpi-6.12 (Raspberry Pi commit `d61c12f36d8d`, PR raspberrypi/linux#6117; mainline defaults to false), and a released output keeps its last level. A crashed `rabbit-safety` would leave ESTOP_RUN high. Fixes: `scripts/body-setup.sh` adds `pinctrl_bcm2835.persist_gpio_outputs=n` to `cmdline.txt`, and `rabbit-safety` reads `/sys/module/pinctrl_bcm2835/parameters/persist_gpio_outputs` at start and fails its self-test (`estop_line_fault`, never raises the line) when it is `Y`. The next layer still holds: roboclaw zeroes the motors 0.4 s after `rabbit.safety.drive` stops, which the e2e test exercises by killing the safety process mid-drive. The stage-2 acceptance test (`docker kill rabbit-safety` → E-stop flag in ≤ 0.1 s) checks it on the real board (V7).

Also from the research: Debian Bookworm's `python3-libgpiod` is the v1 API; the code uses the v2 API (`gpiod.request_lines`, `LineSettings`, `wait_edge_events`), from PyPI `gpiod` ≥ 2.2 (aarch64 wheels) in the body image and the host venv, or Debian Trixie.

## Power supervisor

States `booting → body_ready` (safety self-test passed) `→ running` (brain heard); `jetson_cycle` (EN off 5 s, then back to `body_ready`) when the brain is silent 120 s while the Jetson draws > 0.3 A, at most twice an hour; `stopping` on a 2 s press, 10 s at ≤ 12.8 V or `rabbit.power.request shutdown`: it latches the safety E-stop, cancels nav, asks for `rabbit.map.save` (only with a live brain) and waits ≤ 10 s for `map.saved`/`map.save_skipped`/`map.save_failed` from rabbit-zed; `jetson_halt`: `rabbit.power.jetson poweroff`, then waits for the Jetson current < 0.3 A for 3 s (without the `jetson_12v` INA channel: 15 s of brain silence), at most 45 s, logged as an error when the timeout cuts power; `jetson_off`: EN off; `pi_halt`: `systemctl poweroff`, after which the kernel's `gpio-poweroff` opens the Pololu switch. A short press blinks the charge (one blink per 20 %); a press already held when the service starts is ignored. It runs on the host (systemd, venv `/opt/rabbit-host`), keeps working without NATS (the waits then time out), and in fake mode never runs `poweroff`. Choice: the automatic Jetson power cycle starts only from `running`, as drawn; a Jetson that never boots needs `power_cycle_jetson` by hand (Q28).

## Fake hardware and the simulator

`RABBIT_HW=fake` selects `FakeGpio` (inputs and outputs bridged to `rabbit.sim.gpio.in/out`), `FakeC1` and `FakeTof`, so every body node runs on the Mac. `scripts/sim.sh` with `SIM_BODY=1` adds the real `safety` and `power` nodes; `rabbit-sim` then publishes `rabbit.lidar.scan` (500 rays per rotation from 10 poses of the last 0.1 s, walls and objects at least as tall as the scan plane), `rabbit.tof` (each zone ray-cast in 3D against the floor and the height of every cell, through the same `frame_message`), presses the bumper GPIO when a cell at least 23 mm high is within 12 mm of a bumper, obeys `rabbit.safety.drive` instead of `rabbit.cmd.*`, and brakes and reports the RoboClaw E-stop flag while the fake E-stop line is low. Without `SIM_BODY` nothing changes, so the existing tests and e2e runs are unaffected. `sim.sh stop` no longer aborts (leaving the stack running) when one node already died.

## Nav and planner

As in `-body.md`, "nav merges the lidar scan with the camera scan for `free_distance` and reversing": nav subscribes to `rabbit.lidar.scan`, de-skews each scan with its pose track into the odometry frame and adds it to the guard's obstacle set while it is younger than 0.3 s, forward and in reverse. The planner keeps lidar hits in a second `ObstacleMemory` (every 2nd scan, ~360 rays, 6 s), separate from the camera's, so lidar rays passing over low furniture don't erase camera hits. Nothing else changed (footprint, reverse-only-into-mapped-cells, costs). Without a lidar no message arrives and nothing changes; all 179 earlier tests pass unchanged.

## Forge

New tables `safety_state` (10 Hz), `power_state` (1 Hz and on change), `lidar_health` and `tof_health` (1 Hz, one row per sensor); `rabbit.safety.estop`, `rabbit.safety.reset` and `rabbit.power.request` go to `command_events`; the new events need nothing (`events`). Not stored: full scans (~2 kB × 10 Hz ≈ 70 MB/h raw, several times today's 15–20 MiB/h), ToF frames, and the 50 Hz `rabbit.safety.drive` (its command is in `safety_state`). Slab `body_safety` (per second: worst mode, reasons, requested vs allowed, clearances, line, bumpers, safety events); `nav_stops` now also lists `safety.limit`, `safety.bump`, `safety.estop*`, `safety.brain_lost`, `safety.roboclaw_lost`, `safety.overcurrent`, `safety.battery_critical`.

## Deploy shape (written, not run)

`workspaces/body/compose.yaml`: NATS hub (JetStream, websocket, leaf listener 7422 bound to 10.77.0.1), `nats-init`, `rabbit-roboclaw` (`/dev/ttyAMA0`), `rabbit-steering`, `rabbit-ina` (`I2C_BUS=1`), `rabbit-lidar`, `rabbit-tof`, `rabbit-safety`, all from one `rabbit-body` image (`docker/Dockerfile.body`: python 3.10 slim, numpy, nats-py, pyserial, smbus2, gpiod, Blinka and PCA9685, `circuitpython-vl53lxcx==0.0.5`; built on the Mac for arm64 like Forge) with the code mounted; settings in `body.env` (starts in shadow with no sensors). `workspaces/jetson/nats-leaf.conf` is the Jetson's future leaf config. `scripts/deploy.sh --body [--build] [services]` (host `RABBIT_BODY_HOST`, default `root@rabbit-body.local`) syncs `body`, `nats` and `rabbit`, recreates the named (default: all `rabbit-*`) containers and reinstalls `rabbit-power.service` when named or by default; checked with stubbed ssh/rsync/docker only. `scripts/body-setup.sh` is the one-time Pi setup: `config.txt` overlays from the wiring doc, `persist_gpio_outputs=n`, serial console off, hardware watchdog 15 s, `eth0` 10.77.0.1/30, chrony server, Docker, the host venv and the power unit.

## Verification

- Robot tests: 222 passed, 4 skipped (179 before; new: `test_lidar.py`, `test_tof.py`, `test_safety_loop.py`, `test_power_supervisor.py`, `test_lidar_nav.py`). They pin the protocol bytes and resync, the C1 handshake against the emulator (rotations of 0.09–0.11 s, ≥ 400 points, masked sector empty), de-skew, floor rejection, a stuck sensor power-cycling the ToF rail exactly once, every safety input tripping (obstacle behind, stale lidar, stale ToF with and without lidar, bumper with the 0.5 m crawl budget, silent brain with a working joystick, stale command, joystick limited like nav, silent RoboClaw dropping the line until 1 s of idle telemetry, stale INA, overcurrent for 2 s, low and critical battery, latched E-stop, power shutdown, shadow mode), the self-test failing without the RoboClaw E-stop flag, the kernel GPIO persistence check, the design's stopping-distance table, the shutdown order, the hung-Jetson timeout, the heartbeat fallback without a current channel, short press and boot-time press, critical battery, the twice-an-hour Jetson power cycle, and lidar reaching nav's reverse check and the planner's memory.
- Closed loop in the unit test: reversing at 0.15 m/s toward a wall stops 5–12 cm from it.
- E2E in the simulator (`RABBIT_E2E=1`, random ports; 3 passed, ~2.5 min): the new `test_reversing_into_a_wall_is_stopped_by_the_body_safety_with_each_rear_sensor` reverses at duty −0.32 (0.15 m/s) toward a wall with lidar and ToF (stop 5.2 cm from the wall, no contact, `safety.limit` at 0.08, 0.06, 0.04 m clearance), with the lidar switched off (`safety.input_stale`, 0.1 m/s cap, stop 5.2 cm, no contact), into a 3 cm threshold that the lidar passes over and the ToF classifies as floor (rear bumper → zero command 0.5–3 ms after the GPIO edge by `ts`, design budget 50 ms; the body touches the threshold and then holds still while reverse is still commanded), and kills the safety process while driving forward at 0.15 m/s: the simulated RoboClaw stops on the 0.4 s command timeout.
- Forge: `pnpm check` green (165 tests); a local writer on the body sim recorded `safety_state` 10 rows/s, `power_state` 1/s, `lidar_health` 1/s and `tof_health` 4/s (fake C1 and ToF nodes), no malformed messages, the events of all four nodes, and `body_safety` replayed the run: the limit and stop at 4 cm clearance, the bumper, a latched HUD E-stop with the line dropping and its reset.
- Cost on the Mac (M-series): safety 2.3 % of a core at 50 Hz, step 0.17 ms mean, 3.4 ms max; power 0.6 %; lidar node 2.8 % against the emulator; tof 1.8 % with fake sensors. `safety_state` takes ~134 B/row in the unmerged 10 s files.
