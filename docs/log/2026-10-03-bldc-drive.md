# 2026-10-03 BLDC drive study

Offline research, no robot. Full report (Russian): `reports/2026-10-03-bldc-drive.md`; chart `reports/media/bldc-drive.svg`, generated by `reports/media/bldc-drive.py` (`uv run docs/reports/media/bldc-drive.py`). Nothing in robot code, Forge or `pcb/` changed.

## Why the drive is slow and loud

- Pololu 37D 70:1 at 12 V: 150 rpm free = 0.59 m/s on 75 mm wheels; stall 2.65 N·m per wheel.
- Traction caps useful torque at ~0.32 N·m per wheel (μ 0.7, 55% of 4.5 kg on the rear axle). The gearbox trades away speed for torque the tyres cannot use.
- At 0.5 m/s the motor turns 8,900 rpm. The fast first stage and the brushes make the noise (Pololu forum: the small fast gears are loud, the slow end is "nearly noiseless"; Pololu offers only 20 kHz PWM).
- No dB measurement of the robot exists yet: phase 0 is a phone spectrum at 30 cm.

## Requirements per wheel

Computed in the chart script.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Continuous torque | 0.2 N·m (rug + 5° ramp + 1 m/s²). Forge cross-check: p99 0.45 A on the 70:1 is ~0.19 N·m |
| Peak torque | 0.6 N·m for 1–2 s |
| Speed | ≥ 460 wheel rpm at 14 V (1.5 m/s manual with regulator headroom), smooth from 0.03 m/s (target 0.01) |
| 15 mm threshold | Not climbable statically with rear drive (pushing the front wheels over needs 26.5 N > 17 N traction). It needs ≥ 0.4–0.5 m/s of momentum |

Autonomous cruise stays at 0.16 m/s until the 2.0 lidar, encoders and EKF exist; ZED pose confidence drops above ~0.2 m/s.

Consequences:
- A 37Dx70L (with encoder) is 70.1 mm long, so two of them cannot sit coaxially in the model. Which motors are fitted is open question M5.
- Coaxial motors (planetary, gimbal) either raise the axle 7 mm or cut into deck 1.

## Options found

Online, prices on 2026-10-03.

- **Direct drive and gimbal motors:** ≤ Ø47 gives ≤ 0.5 N·m peak; Ø53–70 does not fit. Integrated planetary actuators (GIM4305-10, AK40-10) are wound for 24 V and reach ~1 m/s on 4S.
- **36 mm planetary BLDC (36GP-3650):**
  - 8 mm shaft on the axis, 450 g;
  - needs a new bracket and a notch in deck 1;
  - listings only show built-in drivers.
- **JGB37-format BLDC gearmotors:** same 37 mm offset spur gearbox, 6 mm D shaft and Ø31 pattern per the listings.
  - JGB37-3650, 12 V, 18.8:1: 1.06 N·m stall, ~US$30.
  - JGB37-3625 is shorter and weaker, 0.37 N·m at 18.8:1.
  - Only built-in-driver versions were confirmed. The bare 3-phase + Hall version and the 7 mm offset must be confirmed with a seller.
- **Pololu 37D 19:1 #4751 / 30:1 #4752:** drop-in, US$60.95 each; 1.57 / 1.08 m/s at 0.2 N·m.

Controllers:
- Sensorless MCF8316 cannot crawl or hold: its BEMF observer hands over at 1–19% of max speed and there is no torque at zero speed.
- Hall-only FOC is rough below ~0.1 m/s. A 14-bit magnetic encoder (MT6701, AS5047P) on the motor shaft fixes that.
- DRV8316C: 4.5–35 V, 95 mΩ, 8 A peak, ~3 A RMS. LCSC C5447274, US$4.30, 2,833 in stock. STM32G474 is C1235412.
- Modules:
  - ODrive Micro (US$89) needs CAN on the Pi; moteus c1 (US$69) needs CAN-FD;
  - SimpleFOC Mini (DRV8316, US$6.90) plus one G474 board runs the same firmware as a future on-board design.

## Recommendation

**Main:**
- JGB37 BLDC, 12 V, 18.8:1, bare with Halls, plus MT6701 on the rear shaft;
- STM32G474 + 2 × DRV8316CR on the body PCB in place of the RoboClaw (~45 × 55 mm);
- SimpleFOC firmware that speaks the RoboClaw packet-serial subset the node already uses (21, 24, 34, 37 speed, 48, 49, 78, 79, 82, 90, 108, 14/15, 20, 133–136), so `roboclaw.py`, `rabbit.roboclaw` and Forge stay as they are;
- ~1.55 m/s at 0.2 N·m, smooth from 0.01 m/s, no chassis change;
- ~US$210 with the prototype.

**Fallback and first step:** Pololu #4751 on the RoboClaw, in speed mode with a 4 A limit, ~US$140. It is also the noise reference against the BLDC.

## Regen

- A 1.5 m/s stop returns up to ~5 J, while 1000–4700 µF absorbs only 0.06–0.28 J between 16.8 and 20 V.
- The 15–25 W electronics load on the same bus normally eats it.
- It is unknown whether the V-mount BMS accepts charge current through the plate.
- Plan:
  1. Firmware cap on negative bus current of 0.25 A per motor; E-stop brakes by shorting the windings.
  2. Comparator + MOSFET + 10 Ω brake chopper at 17.6 V.
  3. Optional ideal diode; SMBJ20A as the last resort.

## Hardware E-stop for rev B

- `ESTOP_RUN` goes to the TIM1/TIM8 BKIN inputs, which force low-side braking in hardware.
- Through an AND gate with "MCU alive" and a 0.3 s RC delay, it also goes to the DRV8316 nSLEEP.

### Battery

NEEWER's spec lists "BP/D Tap: 16.8V 8A (Max)" as inputs and "Fully charged in 3h via a 16.8V D Tap charger". The plate accepts charge on paper. BMS behaviour at full charge and on current spikes is unknown, so the design stays "does not accept":
- 0.5 A regen cap in firmware;
- 17.6 V chopper;
- the ideal diode is not needed.

### PCB rev B requirements

Report §8, written for the PCB agent. Stock is from the JLCPCB catalogue API.

**Motor block:**
- STM32G474RET6 LQFP-64 (C521608, 242 in stock). The QFN-48 lacks pins: the budget is 49 of 52. The G431RBT6 (C431633) is the backup.
- 2× DRV8316CRRGFR (C5447274, 2,833) in 3x PWM mode.
- E-stop: TIM1/TIM8 BKIN brakes in hardware; a NAND of `ESTOP_RUN` and MCU-alive drives DRVOFF. DRVOFF rather than nSLEEP, which resets the registers.
- Brake chopper: TLV3201 (C105188) → AO3400A (C20917, base) → 4× 56 Ω 2 W 2512 (C2912630).
- MCU powered from the board 5 V through TLV75533 (C404027), not from the DRV8316 buck.

**MCU takes over:**
- Servo PWM from TIM15/16/17 with SN74AHCT1G125 (C7484) buffers, dropping the PCA9685. The PCA9685 is in stock anyway: C92206, 1,205.
- Bumpers.

**Modules → JLC parts:**
- TPS56637 (C841386) for the 5 V and 6 V rails.
- Jetson fed straight from the bus through a TPS259474 eFuse (C2864845); the carrier input is 9–20 V per its spec, J16.
- LTC2954 (C683782) + LM74502 (C3236215) + 2× CSD17573Q5B (C202231) instead of the Pololu #2813 switch.
- JLC assembles SMT and THT (wave) on both sides.

## Second update: the short motor is the primary

The coordinator asked whether the 3650 is needed at all, given the ~0.32 N·m grip limit and the 58 mm per side in the chassis model. Recomputed at 4S: 14 V under load, 95% voltage use in FOC, gearbox efficiency 0.8.

Assumption for the 3625: phase resistance was estimated as ~6.7 Ω line-to-line from XYT's 12 V stall current of 1.8 A. The winding then caps the current near 2 A. This must be verified on the bench.

| Option | Length (gearbox + encoder cap) | Free speed | At 0.05 N·m (rug) | At 0.13 N·m | Peak at the wheel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XYT JGB37-3625-1260, 12 V, 500 rpm/V, 10:1 | ~53 mm | 2.61 m/s | 2.18 m/s | 1.49 m/s | ~0.30 N·m |
| Same, 18.8:1 | | 1.39 m/s | | | ~0.57 N·m |
| Chihai 3650, 6.25:1 | ~76 mm (18 mm over) | 2.89 m/s | | 2.48 m/s | 0.69 N·m |

The 3625 at 10:1 covers:
- rug cruise up to ~2.2 m/s;
- a 5° ramp at about the rated 0.8 A;
- holding on 5° at ~0.45 A;
- acceleration up to about the grip limit at low speed.

The 15 mm threshold needs momentum (≥ 0.5 m/s), which the front axle needs anyway.

**Verdict:** the 3650 is not needed. It only adds speed under load and a static threshold climb, and it does not fit.

Changes:
- **Encoder:** the XYT driver board carries the Halls, so the angle comes from the MT6701 over SSI (absolute). Its magnet sits on the rear shaft stub, which shows on the XYT rotor photo, the Chihai GM37-BL3525 photo and the MY-BL3525 drawing. Encoders on the gearbox output or the wheel hub are not practical: there is a 3.3 mm gap and nothing stationary outside the wheel. Hall-only with SimpleFOC `SmoothingSensor` gives 2 mm steps per edge at 10:1 and needs a bare motor with Halls.
- **Report §8, forced by the motor:**
